# SOP — Colorimetric L-Lactate Assay for Bacterial Supernatants

## 2. Additional things required from normal lab stock

- Tris base
- HCl for adjusting Tris to pH 8.2
- Methanol
- DMSO; ordinary laboratory/reagent-grade DMSO is sufficient, anhydrous DMSO is not specified by the protocol
- ultrapure/distilled water
- the same uninoculated bacterial culture medium used for the experiment
- clear flat-bottom 384-well assay plates
- plate reader capable of A490
- centrifuge for clearing bacterial cultures -> 4min max speed, turn on cooling 0C 10min before use
- calibrated pipettes/multichannel or liquid handler
- small aliquot tubes
- foil/light protection

The published protocol specifies 0.2 M Tris pH 8.2, 22 mg/mL β-NAD⁺, 10 mg/mL INT in 67% methanol, 25 mg/mL methoxy-PMS in DMSO, 1 M sodium L-lactate and 1 M acetic acid.

# PART A — One-time stock preparation

## A1. 0.2 M Tris, pH 8.2

For **500 mL**:

1. Weigh **12.114 g Tris base**.
2. Dissolve in approximately 400–450 mL ultrapure water.
3. Adjust to **pH 8.20 at room temperature** with HCl.
4. Bring to exactly 500 mL with ultrapure water.
5. Store as normal for your laboratory's Tris buffers.

This is the assay's main buffer. The published method uses 0.2 M Tris at pH 8.2.

---

## A2. β-NAD⁺ stock — 22 mg/mL

Do **not** dissolve the entire 1 g bottle.

For a convenient **10 mL stock**:

1. Weigh **220 mg N0632 β-NAD⁺ sodium salt**.
2. Dissolve in ultrapure water.
3. Bring to **10.00 mL final volume**.
4. Mix gently.
5. Divide into approximately 0.5–1 mL aliquots.
6. Store at **−20 °C**.

Final:

**22 mg/mL β-NAD⁺**

The protocol specifically requires N0632 at this concentration and stores NAD⁺ aliquots at −20 °C.

---

## A3. INT stock — 10 mg/mL in 67% methanol

Again, do not dissolve the entire 1 g package.

Prepare **10 mL**:

1. Measure **6.7 mL methanol**.
2. Add **3.3 mL ultrapure water**.
3. Add **100 mg INT**.
4. Mix until dissolved.
5. Protect from strong light.
6. Aliquot.
7. Store at **−20 °C**.

Final:

**10 mg/mL INT in 67% MeOH**

The protocol specifies exactly this stock concentration and solvent composition.

---

## A4. Methoxy-PMS stock — 25 mg/mL in DMSO

You have a 10 mg powder vial.

For the whole vial:

1. Add DMSO and bring to **400 µL final volume**.
2. Mix gently until completely dissolved.
3. Make small aliquots, e.g. **10–20 µL**.
4. Protect from light.
5. Store at **−20 °C**.

Calculation:

10 mg / 0.400 mL = **25 mg/mL**

The published protocol uses 25 mg/mL methoxy-PMS in DMSO.

**Important safety note:** the Biomol/TargetMol product is classified H351, i.e. suspected of causing cancer, so it still belongs under your laboratory's chemical-risk/SDS handling procedures even though the quantities used are very small.

---

## A5. Sodium L-lactate stock — 1 M

You have approximately 25 mg sodium L-lactate.

Molecular weight:

**112.06 g/mol**

At exactly 25.00 mg and 100% purity:

25 mg / 112.06 mg/mmol = 0.2231 mmol

Therefore the final volume for a 1 M stock is:

**223 µL**

Procedure:

1. Check the actual mass/purity from the vial or CoA.
2. Dissolve the sodium L-lactate in ultrapure water.
3. Bring to the calculated final volume corresponding to **1.000 M**.
4. Mix thoroughly.
5. Divide into small aliquots.
6. Store at **−20 °C**.

The protocol specifies a 1 M sodium L-lactate stock stored in aliquots at −20 °C.

### Make a convenient 100 mM intermediate

On an assay day:

- 20 µL 1 M sodium L-lactate
- 180 µL culture medium

= **200 µL of 100 mM L-lactate**

This makes preparation of the low-mM calibration standards much easier.

---

## A6. 1 M acetic acid stop solution

Your VWR stock is approximately 99–100% acetic acid, approximately 17.4 M.

For **100 mL of 1 M acetic acid**:

1. Place approximately 80 mL ultrapure water into a suitable vessel.
2. Add approximately **5.75 mL concentrated acetic acid to the water**.
3. Mix.
4. Bring to exactly **100 mL final volume** with water.

Final:

**1 M acetic acid**

Use the actual concentration/purity stated on the bottle/CoA for the final calculation if you want maximal accuracy.

This solution is added only after the one-hour incubation to stop the reaction. The published assay uses 1 M acetic acid for this purpose.

---

# PART B — L1254 enzyme preparation

The original protocol did **not** use L1254. It used Roche 10127230001:

- rabbit muscle
- 5 mg/mL
- approximately 550 U/mg protein at 25 °C.

Thus the original liquid enzyme contains approximately:

5 mg/mL × 550 U/mg = **2,750 U/mL = 2.75 U/µL**.

Your L1254 is also rabbit-muscle L-LDH, but it is lyophilized and its specific activity is 600–1,200 U/mg. One L1254 unit is defined by Sigma as reduction of 1 µmol pyruvate/min at pH 7.5 and 37 °C.

Therefore, rather than trying to reproduce 5 mg/mL by mass, prepare an **activity-matched working stock of 2.75 U/µL**.

## If your vial is 5 KU

5 KU = **5,000 U**

Required final stock volume:

5,000 U / 2.75 U/µL = **1,818 µL**

Therefore:

1. Reconstitute the 5 KU L1254 vial and bring to approximately **1.82 mL final volume** with a neutral phosphate buffer, e.g. 10 mM phosphate buffer around pH 7.4.
2. Mix gently; do not vigorously vortex the enzyme.
3. Divide into small aliquots, e.g. 20–50 µL.
4. Store at **−20 °C**.
5. Avoid repeated freeze–thaw cycles.

Final nominal activity:

**2.75 U/µL**

If the actual vial says 1 KU instead:

1,000 U / 2.75 U/µL = **364 µL**

Use the activity on the vial/CoA rather than assuming the package size. Sigma specifies L1254 as a lyophilized rabbit-muscle Type XI LDH stored at −20 °C.

---

# PART C — Which LDH concentration to use

The ordinary published assay uses:

**1 µL of the original 2.75 U/µL LDH stock per ~5 mL assay buffer**

≈ **2.75 U per 5 mL**

However, the authors found inhibition when their medium contained **1 mM sodium pyruvate** and increased the enzyme sixfold:

**6 µL LDH per ~5 mL assay buffer**

≈ **16.5 U per 5 mL**

They also changed the plate-reader setting in this condition from A490−A650 to **A490 without the 650-nm reference**.

Because this experiment uses **bacterial culture supernatants where pyruvate concentrations are not known beforehand**, use the **high-LDH formulation for the initial validation**.

If it performs well, use that same formulation consistently for standards and samples throughout the screen.

---

# PART D — Standard preparation

The published calibration range is:

- 0 mM — medium blank
- 0.375 mM
- 0.75 mM
- 1.5 mM
- 3 mM
- 6 mM
- 12 mM L-lactate

Standards must be made in the **same culture medium used for the bacterial experiment**, not simply in water.

Prepare approximately 100 µL of each standard in a separate tube or source reservoir. This is
the total prepared amount, **not the volume placed in one 384-well assay well**; dispense only
15 µL per assigned assay well.

## Efficient serial preparation

Prepare **200 µL of 12 mM**:

- 24 µL of 100 mM intermediate
- 176 µL culture medium

Then put **100 µL culture medium** into five tubes labelled:

- 6 mM
- 3 mM
- 1.5 mM
- 0.75 mM
- 0.375 mM

Serially dilute:

1. Transfer 100 µL 12 mM → 6 mM tube; mix.
2. Transfer 100 µL 6 mM → 3 mM tube; mix.
3. Transfer 100 µL 3 mM → 1.5 mM tube; mix.
4. Transfer 100 µL 1.5 mM → 0.75 mM tube; mix.
5. Transfer 100 µL 0.75 mM → 0.375 mM tube; mix.

Use untreated culture medium as the **0 mM blank**.

This consumes only:

**2.4 µL of the original 1 M stock per complete standard series.**

Your 25 mg sodium L-lactate is therefore enough for many dozens of plates.

---

# PART E — Published 96-well assay buffer

For reference, the published normal formulation is:

- 4,250 µL 0.2 M Tris, pH 8.2
- 500 µL NAD⁺ stock
- 250 µL INT stock
- 1 µL original LDH stock
- 1.1 µL methoxy-PMS stock

The pyruvate/high-LDH version changes only the enzyme:

- **6 µL LDH instead of 1 µL**

Everything else remains the same.

---

# PART F — 384-well version for this experiment

The original protocol is a **150 µL final-volume 96-well assay**:

- 50 µL sample
- 50 µL assay buffer
- 50 µL 1 M acetic acid.



For the intended 40–50 µL 384-well format, use a **0.3× proportional scale**:

### Per well

- **15 µL sample / standard / blank**
- **15 µL assay buffer**
- incubate 1 h
- **15 µL 1 M acetic acid**

Final volume:

**45 µL**

All reagent concentrations and ratios remain the same as in the published assay; only absolute volumes are reduced.

Because the publication validated 96-well/150-µL operation rather than 384-well/45-µL operation, validate the 45-µL format before processing hundreds of cultures.

---

# PART G — Assay buffer for one full 384-well plate

A full plate requires:

384 × 15 µL = **5.76 mL assay buffer**

Prepare approximately **6.4 mL**, providing roughly 10% excess for pipetting/dead volume.

Using the **high-LDH version**:

- **5,440 µL** 0.2 M Tris, pH 8.2
- **640 µL** β-NAD⁺ stock, 22 mg/mL
- **320 µL** INT stock, 10 mg/mL in 67% MeOH
- **7.68 µL** L1254 stock, 2.75 U/µL
- **1.41 µL** methoxy-PMS stock, 25 mg/mL

Mix gently.

Protect the completed assay buffer from strong light and prepare it fresh for the plate.

---

# PART H — Sample preparation

1. Grow the bacterial communities under the planned experimental conditions.
2. At the assay time point, centrifuge cultures sufficiently to pellet the bacterial cells.
3. Transfer clear supernatant without disturbing the pellet.
4. If necessary, dilute the supernatant in the **same uninoculated culture medium**.
5. Choose a dilution that places the resulting L-lactate concentration inside the **0.375–12 mM calibration range**.
6. Record the dilution factor.

For the current fermentation screen (maximum theoretical L-lactate 32.6 mM), use **1:3** as
the routine starting dilution: **1 volume supernatant + 2 volumes uninoculated screen medium**.
This places even the theoretical maximum at 10.9 mM, inside the 0.375–12 mM calibration range,
while avoiding unnecessary dilution of the expected samples. Re-run below-range samples
undiluted.

If only approximately 40 µL original supernatant is available, dilution is particularly useful:

- 1:2 dilution produces approximately 80 µL
- enough for three 15-µL technical replicates plus some pipetting margin.

The published protocol explicitly permits dilution of samples when concentrations exceed its calibration range.

---

# PART I — Actual plate assay

## Plate setup

Run at least:

- culture-medium blank
- 0.375 mM standard
- 0.75 mM
- 1.5 mM
- 3 mM
- 6 mM
- 12 mM
- bacterial supernatants

Use **2–3 technical replicates**.

For the first validation plate, use three replicates for the standards.

## Procedure

1. Add **15 µL** culture medium to blank wells.
2. Add **15 µL** of each L-lactate standard to its assigned wells.
3. Add **15 µL** diluted bacterial supernatant to sample wells.
4. Add **15 µL freshly prepared assay buffer** to every well.
5. Mix gently and consistently.
6. Cover the plate.
7. Incubate for **1 hour at room temperature in the dark**.
8. Add **15 µL 1 M acetic acid** to every well.
9. Mix gently.
10. Remove or avoid bubbles before reading.
11. Read absorbance at **490 nm**.

Because this SOP intentionally uses the protocol's high-LDH/pyruvate condition, use **A490 without the 650-nm reference channel** initially, matching the published pyruvate condition.

---

# PART J — Mandatory first validation before the 400–800-community screen

Do not immediately start with hundreds of communities.

First run the complete standard curve in the 45-µL 384-well format.

Verify:

- blank is low and stable
- replicate wells agree
- absorbance increases monotonically with L-lactate concentration
- the useful calibration range covers approximately 0.375–12 mM
- the 12 mM standard has not saturated the plate reader
- the 1-hour incubation gives a stable result.

Because bacterial metabolites may differ from the cell-culture environment used in the original publication, also perform a **spike-recovery check** on several representative bacterial supernatants:

1. Measure an unspiked aliquot.
2. Add a known quantity of L-lactate to a second aliquot.
3. Measure both.
4. Confirm that the increase measured by the assay approximately matches the amount added.

If sodium pyruvate is available in the lab, an additional useful validation is to run fixed L-lactate standards with increasing pyruvate concentrations. The publication only directly demonstrated its sixfold-LDH workaround for **1 mM pyruvate**, so higher bacterial pyruvate concentrations should not be assumed automatically to be harmless.

---

# PART K — Data analysis

For each plate:

1. Calculate the mean blank absorbance.
2. Subtract the mean blank from all standards and samples.
3. Fit the L-lactate calibration curve.
4. Convert each sample absorbance into L-lactate concentration from that curve.
5. Multiply by the sample dilution factor.

Example:

Measured 1:3-diluted sample:

**4.2 mM**

Correct for the 1:3 dilution:

4.2 × 3 = **12.6 mM L-lactate in the original supernatant**

If desired in g/L:

12.6 mmol/L × 90.08 mg/mmol = **1.14 g/L L-lactic acid equivalent**

The published protocol likewise uses blank subtraction followed by calibration-curve interpolation to calculate L-lactate concentration.

---

# Practical workflow summary

### One time

Prepare and aliquot:

- Tris pH 8.2
- NAD⁺
- INT
- methoxy-PMS
- sodium L-lactate
- L1254 enzyme stock
- 1 M acetic acid

### Each assay day

Prepare:

- fresh working L-lactate standards
- diluted culture supernatants
- fresh assay master mix

Then:

**15 µL sample + 15 µL assay mix → 1 h RT dark → 15 µL 1 M acetic acid → A490 → blank subtraction → standard curve → dilution correction.**

That is the complete working assay.
