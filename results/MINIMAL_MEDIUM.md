# GEM minimal medium, requirements and provisions — pool v2

Generated by `minimal_medium.py`. Tables: `minimal_medium.tsv`,
`provision_matrix.tsv`, `exchangeable_mechanisms.tsv`.

## Headline

**All nine models grow on glycerol plus mineral salts with zero required
supplements.** So the answer to "what is the most minimal possible medium" is, in
silico: mineral salts + glycerol, for every member of the pool.

That flatly contradicts the annotation prior, which called `P3_B12` an auxotroph
for eight pathways and both Gluconobacters auxotrophs for three. The disagreement
has a single root cause, and it is not that the annotation was wrong.

## Why the models say prototroph: the biomass equation omits the cofactors

CarveMe's biomass reaction is **57 metabolites and byte-identical across all nine
models**. It contains CoA, thiamine diphosphate, pyridoxal-5-phosphate, THF,
riboflavin, FAD, NAD and methionine. It does **not** contain:

> biotin · pantothenate · lipoate · heme · folate

Nothing demands them, so their biosynthesis is never essential and their absence can
never register as a requirement. **For biotin, pantothenate, lipoate, heme and
folate the GEM cannot adjudicate auxotrophy at all** — the annotation prior is the
only evidence available, and it should not be treated as overruled here.

The GEM *can* adjudicate thiamine, pyridoxal, riboflavin, methionine and NAD,
because those are in biomass.

A targeted check on where the model's route lacks gene support partially corroborates
the annotation:

| Genome | Annotation said | Synthesis evidence-free in model | Transport-only in model |
|---|---|---|---|
| `P3_B12` | 8 pathways | pantothenate, pyridoxal | biotin, folate, lipoate |
| `P3_C11` | methionine, pantothenate, quinone | pantothenate | folate |
| `P3_C9` | methionine, pantothenate, quinone | none | none |
| `P3_F11` | biotin | pyridoxal | folate, lipoate |
| `P1_B11` | none | pyridoxal | folate, lipoate |

`P3_B12`'s only gene-backed producers of biotin are `BTNt2i` and `BTNt`, both
**transporters** — it can take biotin up but not make it, which agrees with the
annotation rather than contradicting it. Conversely `P3_C9` shows no evidence-free
or transport-only gaps, so its three annotation-predicted requirements are probably
naming artefacts.

Caveat on this table: producers of a terminal metabolite include salvage and
peptidase reactions, so "gene-supported" means "can obtain", not "can synthesise de
novo". A pathway-level GPR audit would be the stronger test.

## Which cross-feeding mechanisms these models can actually test

A cross-feed needs a supplier **and** a receiver that benefits. Checking both kills
most of the plan:

| Mechanism | Secretors | Benefit possible | Testable | Blocked by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **lactate production** | `P1_B3` `P1_B11` `P3_F11` `P3_C11` `P3_C9` | product | **yes** | |
| **methionine** | `P2_D12` | 9/9 demand | **yes** | |
| **DHA transfer** | `P2_D12` | `P3_C9`, `P2_D12` grow on it | **yes** | |
| **G3P transfer** | `P2_D12` | 6/9 grow on it | **yes** | |
| **amino acids** (Ile, Leu, Lys, Val, Arg, and more from `P2_D12`) | most members | 9/9 | **yes** | |
| biotin rescue | none | 0/9 demand | no | not secretable, not demanded |
| thiamine B1 | none | 9/9 demand | no | not secretable |
| folate B9 | none | 9/9 demand | no | not secretable |
| riboflavin B2 | none | 9/9 demand | no | not secretable |
| pantothenate | none | 9/9 demand | no | not secretable |
| lipoate | none | 0/9 demand | no | not secretable, not demanded |
| heme rescue | `P1_B3` `P1_B11` `P2_D12` `P3_A10` `P3_B12` | **0/9 demand** | no | biomass demands no form of it |
| pyridoxal B6 | `P3_F11` at 1e-4 | 9/9 demand | nominal only | flux too small to matter |

**Vitamin secretion is structurally impossible, not merely unfavoured.** With the
growth constraint removed entirely, max secretion of biotin, thiamine, riboflavin,
pantothenate, folate and lipoate is exactly **0** in every model. No constraint
tuning will produce these cross-feeds. Heme is the exception — 5 models secrete it at
0.5–0.83 — but nothing demands it.

### Validated against the genomes: this is not a CarveMe bias

The obvious suspicion is that CarveMe dropped exporters the genomes encode. It did
not. **Every annotated cofactor transporter in all nine genomes is an uptake system:**

| Transporter | Function | Found in |
|---|---|---|
| `bioY`, `bioM`, `bioN` | ECF biotin **uptake** S-component | `P1_B3`, `P1_B11`, `P3_F11`, `P3_B12`, `P1_G5` |
| `thiT`, `thiW`, `thiP`, `thiQ`, `ykoF` | thiamine **import** (ThiBPQ ABC, YuaJ) | 6 of 9 |
| `ribU`, `hmpT` | ECF riboflavin **uptake** | `P1_B3`, `P1_B11`, `P3_F11`, `P3_B12` |
| `panT`, `panF` | pantothenate **uptake** symporter | `P3_B12`, `P2_D12` |
| `lipT` | lipoate **uptake** | `P2_D12` |
| `folT` | folate uptake | none |

No vitamin efflux system is annotated in any of them. The models are faithful to the
genomes, and the annotation's auxotrophy calls are corroborated: these strains are
vitamin **consumers**.

**The real block is biosynthesis, not transport.** Biotin export transport exists in
**all nine models** (`in/out`), yet max biotin secretion is still 0 — so the models
simply cannot make surplus biotin. Correcting an earlier reading of this: adding
export transport would not help, because there is nothing to export.

Heme is the one genuine exception on the supply side, and the genomes back it:
`hrtAB`, a documented heme efflux pump, is annotated in `P1_B3` and `P3_A10`, and
those models do secrete heme.

### What that means for the vitamin cross-feeding hypothesis

It is not refuted — it is outside what this formalism can express. In real
communities vitamin release is largely **cell lysis and membrane leakage** (public
goods from dead cells), not dedicated efflux. Neither is in a GEM, and neither is in
dFBA. So the hypothesis can be true in the flask while being structurally absent from
every model, and no amount of better annotation closes that gap. Representing it
would mean adding a leakage sink as an explicit modelling assumption, not a curation
from evidence.

### The productive fix: amend the biomass equation

The uptake side already works — vitamin import transport is present in most models.
So if the **biomass equation demanded** biotin, pantothenate, folate, lipoate and
heme at physiologically small coefficients, then:

- the annotation-supported auxotrophies would become real in silico,
- medium supplementation would rescue them through transport that already exists,
- and `hrtAB`-backed heme provision by `P1_B3`/`P3_A10` would become a testable
  community mechanism instead of a supply with no demand.

This is one targeted, literature-anchored edit that unlocks four of the mechanisms
the pool was designed around. It is the clearest case in this project for amending a
CarveMe default rather than working around it.

Vitamins would then function as a **medium axis** rather than a community axis, which
is also the economically relevant framing: you would buy biotin, not engineer a
biotin-secreting partner.

So of the mechanisms the pool was designed around, **biotin rescue, heme rescue,
lipoate supply and B12 provision are all untestable as built.**

## `P2_D12` is the metabolic hub, and it eats the product

Every testable nutritional cross-feed except B6 runs through `P2_D12`, which secretes
alanine, aspartate, cysteine, glutamate, glycine, serine, threonine, tryptophan,
phenylalanine, methionine, succinate, citrate, DHA and G3P — and consumes lactate.
The one strain that can nutritionally support the pool is the one that costs titre.
That tension is now a measured fact rather than a hypothesis, and it is the sharpest
thing the presence/absence landscape can test.

## Lactate is not in conflict with growth

The production envelope is favourable — lactate and biomass largely co-produce:

| Genome | µ max | 0% µ | 25% µ | 50% µ | 75% µ | 100% µ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `P1_B3` | 0.117 | 1.00 | 0.96 | 0.92 | 0.88 | **0.82** |
| `P1_B11` | 0.129 | 1.00 | 0.95 | 0.91 | 0.86 | **0.81** |
| `P3_F11` | 0.121 | 1.00 | 0.95 | 0.91 | 0.86 | **0.81** |
| `P3_C11` | 0.128 | 1.00 | 0.95 | 0.91 | 0.86 | **0.78** |
| `P3_C9` | 0.100 | 1.00 | 0.95 | 0.89 | 0.84 | **0.70** |

(mol L-lactate per mol glycerol at each enforced fraction of maximum growth)

Even at full growth the yield is 0.70–0.82 mol/mol. A growth-maximising dFBA will
therefore still make lactate, which is what COMETS needs to be informative.

**But formate and ethanol compete hard.** At half-maximal growth the same strains
secrete formate at 10–18.8 and ethanol at 3–10.7 mmol/gDW/h against lactate at
~9.1. Carbon is being split three ways, so the realistic yield in a
growth-maximising simulation will sit well below the envelope ceiling.

## Consequences for the next steps

1. **The baseline landscape is worth running as-is** for lactate, amino acid
   cross-feeding, DHA and G3P transfer. Those are real and measured.
2. **Four designed mechanisms need curation before they can be tested**: vitamin
   export transport (biotin, thiamine, folate, riboflavin, pantothenate), heme demand
   in the biomass equation, and B12/quinone exchange reactions. Each is a defensible
   edit, but each is an edit.
3. **The medium should follow the annotation, not the model**, for biotin,
   pantothenate, lipoate, heme and folate — the model is silent on those by
   construction, not permissive.
4. **Formate and ethanol must be in the product labels**, not just lactate, or the
   carbon balance will be unaccounted for.
